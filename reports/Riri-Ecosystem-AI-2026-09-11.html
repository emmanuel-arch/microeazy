One Riri

There are four Riris in the estate today, three corpora, two embedding models and two incompatible ways of scoping a tenant — and the one person who actually waits for an answer, the customer, has no assistant at all. This is the plan to collapse that into one brain with five contracts, put her in front of every system including a lender's own, and let anyone train her on their JSON without ever letting that JSON invent a button.

The three things that will bite if they are not fixed first

  • A shadow copy of the retrieval code, disagreeing with the live one. Corrected 11 Sep This page first reported a live embedding mismatch. That was wrong, and the way it was wrong is the finding. src/data/kb/ holds committed copies of rag.ts, seed-kb.ts and route.ts that name text-embedding-004; the real files under src/lib/ and src/scripts/ both use gemini-embedding-001 at 768 and always did — as does the C# seeder. Nothing is broken. But that folder is not a scratch drawer: its *.json files are the live corpus, read by the seeder at src/data/kb, so live data and a stale shadow of the retrieval code sit in one directory. It is exactly where a person or a model looks when asked to read the knowledge base, and what is sitting there is a plausible, out-of-date account of how retrieval works. I read it first and believed it. The .ts/.tsx files there are referenced by nothing and are recoverable from git — delete them.
  • Two tenancy conventions. The C# side scopes by entity_id INTEGER (0 = shared, N = lender). The console scopes by orgId uuid. A shared corpus cannot have two primary keys, and member codes already collide — EntityId 3003 is “Micromart Check-off” on one server and “Axe — Boresha” on the other (Tailnet Cutover, §NEXT). Key the corpus on the same thing the Interchange will key members on.
  • The substring scope check. AiIntelligenceController admits a generated query if the SQL merely contains the entity id as text. EntityId = 300 passes the check for entity 3002 because “3002” appears in a date, a limit, an id. Ten unrelated companies share the .198 book separated by nothing but EntityId. This is the single highest-severity item on the page.

Found while building Sprint 0 — our own book, not Micromart's

The console's tenant isolation is not in force in this environment, and every visible signal says it is. Loan, Borrower and the rest have rowsecurity AND forcerowsecurity true, the tenant_isolation policy is correct, all fourteen riri_* views are security_invoker=true, and readpath.ts correctly stamps app.org_id on its own transaction. None of it applies: the app connects as postgres, and that role has rolbypassrls = true.

FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY does not stop a BYPASSRLS role. FORCE closes the table-owner exemption; BYPASSRLS is a separate and higher one, and policies are not consulted at all. The measured effect: npm run test:riri fails four assertions in its RLS section — a freshly-created fixture org reads 515 loans instead of its own 4, and another org's balance is visible to it. readpath.ts already carries the warning in a comment: "the replica URL MUST be the lms_app role (NOBYPASSRLS) — a replica reached as postgres would serve every tenant's book to every tenant." The role exists in the design and is not the one in use here.

Confirmed on the live database, 11 Sep 2026. The connection authenticates as the pooler user postgres.<project-ref>, which is the postgres role, and it carries rolbypassrls. This is not a local-environment artefact.

The fix is written and not run: prisma/rls-app-role.sql creates the lms_app role the design already names, and npm run db:rls:role proves isolation against a candidate connection string before DATABASE_URL moves onto it. Pre-flight measured the blast radius as small: 61 RLS tables and every one has a policy, the five without RLS are exactly the set rls.sql documents as deliberate, and the only two code paths that bypass the tenant stamp both touch RateLimit, which has RLS off. Creating a role and rotating the runtime credential on a live book is the founder's to execute.

The honest summary

The console already contains the best assistant architecture in the estate by a wide margin — a host seam, a pure router with a test, a system map that cannot drift, a bilingual corpus that will not compile if a screen is deleted. The work is not to design something new. It is to lift that out of one app and make it the thing all six systems and every partner uses — and to stop the other three from answering at all until they do.

02

Riri Core — one brain, five contracts

The package is extracted from connected-suite/src/lib/riri and imports nothing from any application. It never touches a database, a session or a framework. Everything it needs about the world arrives through five typed contracts, and a host implements them. That sentence is the whole product: “add Riri to your system” must not mean “migrate your book to our database first”.

1 · Host

EXISTS — lib/riri/host.ts

Who is asking, who they are looking at, what this book contains, what Riri concluded before, and how she writes it down. Already implemented by providers/lms.ts. Needs two more: servicesuite.ts (over the read-only relay) and portal.ts (the customer, one row — their own).

2 · Context

NEW — lib/riri/context descriptor

Where she is standing: system, surface, screen, subject, language, rights, entitlements, tenant. Every field server-resolved. The client may name an id; it may never state a fact. That rule is already written and enforced in subject.ts — this generalises it to the whole descriptor.

3 · Corpus

TWO TIERS, ONE RETRIEVER

Our knowledge is code and cannot drift. Their knowledge is JSON and cannot describe a screen. §04 is the line between them, and it is what makes “train it on your own data” safe to sell.

4 · Tools

THE ONLY ROUTE TO A FACT

Every live number comes from a tool call. Consent, rights and tenancy are checked inside the tool body, never in the prompt — Blueprint v2 §6.2, and the first thing a security reviewer will test.

5 · Destination

NEW — the federated map

Each system publishes a manifest of its screens in the shape system-map.ts already uses. Autopilot resolves a sentence to { system, href } across the whole estate, and still stops at the door (§06).

The engines, unchanged

support · assistant · analytics

Behind one conversation, picked by the pure router from the finished question, and stamped on the answer afterwards. Nobody chooses an engine. Nobody ever should have had to.

What the seam looks like on the wire

A partner does not import TypeScript. They get one signed HTTP contract and a web component, which is the same shape the Interchange already uses for consent — one integration pattern across the whole ecosystem rather than a new one per product.

Why the signature and not an API key

The relay already proves this pattern works across a hostile boundary — HMAC over the exact body inside a two-minute window, in lib/enterprise/relay.ts. Reusing it means a partner integration and a lender's own book are protected by the same reviewed code, and there is one place to fix it rather than two.

03

Context — how she knows where she is standing

“Different contexts, recognise the different screens.” The console already does the hard half: the browser names a subject by id and the server states every fact about them. What is missing is that the screen is not yet part of the question, so an officer looking at a loan still has to say which loan.

Screen awareness, concretely

Every screen in every system already declares purpose, does[], implications[] and asks[] in the map. Passing the current route in the descriptor turns that into three things nobody has today:

  • “What am I looking at?” answered from the screen's own entry, including the consequences — the half nobody documents and everybody needs.
  • “Why can't I do this?” answered from the difference between the screen's right/feature and the actor's — so the answer is “your role doesn't hold loans.approve, ask your branch manager”, not a walkthrough they will fail halfway through.
  • The proactive line. On a screen with a known trap, she can open with it. The map already carries the traps; nothing new has to be written.
04

The corpus — ours in code, theirs in JSON

This is the section that makes “put our model in your system and train it with your own JSON” a product rather than a liability. Two tiers, one retriever, and a hard line between them that is enforced by the shape of the data, not by a prompt.

Tier A · The platform corpus ours

CODE · COMPILE-CHECKED · CANNOT DRIFT

knowledge.ts articles, system-map.ts screens and concepts, catalog.ts metrics. An href is verified against the real route table; a right is typed against the real rights catalogue. Delete a screen and the build fails. This is the only tier permitted to produce a navigation action.

Tier B · The tenant corpus theirs

JSON · EMBEDDED · VERSIONED · REVOCABLE

A lender's products, fees, eligibility, timelines, branch hours, policies — and uploaded documents. Answers what they are true about. It can carry an external URL. It can never carry an in-app destination, so a tenant pack that is wrong sends somebody to the wrong paragraph, never to a button that does not exist.

One pack schema, everywhere

The Hub's KB files, the C# SsKbEntry and the SeedLenderKnowledge body are already three dialects of the same thing. Canonicalise once — this is the file a partner writes, drops in, and is trained on. 

{
  "pack":      "micromart.products",     // stable id; re-upload replaces this pack only
  "owner":     "KE/LENDER/P051234567X",  // KRA PIN, not EntityId (§01)
  "version":   7,
  "audience":  ["customer","staff"],     // who may retrieve it
  "lang":      "en",
  "authority": "tenant",                 // tenant | platform | document
  "entries": [{
    "category": "products",
    "question": "What loan products do you offer?",
    "variants": ["products gani", "what can I borrow", "nipe products zenu"],
    "answer":   "...",
    "url":      "https://micromart.co.ke/products",   // EXTERNAL ONLY — never an app route
    "effective_from": "2026-09-01",
    "review_by":      "2026-12-01"       // an unreviewed fee answer is a wrong fee answer
  }]
}

The rule that keeps Tier B safe

A tenant pack may state a fact about the tenant. It may never state a fact about the software. Enforced at ingest: an entry whose url is relative, or whose text matches a navigation phrasing, is rejected with a reason. It is also why the answer carries sources[] with the pack and version — when a lender says “Riri told my customer the wrong fee”, the answer is a pack id, a version and a date, not an argument.

Documents are a third authority, not a fourth corpus

The Ratiba agreement, Blueprint v2, the Tailnet Cutover, the operations playbook — these are chunked into the same table with authority: "document" and a page anchor. The difference is in how she is allowed to use them: a document chunk may be quoted and cited, never paraphrased into a figure. “The agreement says Safaricom handles activation and transaction-status queries (§6.4)” is a good answer. “Safaricom will fix your Ratiba in 24 hours” is not, and nothing in the document supports it.

05

Live data — tools, not prompts

Blueprint v2 §6 settled the strategy and it is right: the moat is the pool and the tools, not the weights. A frontier model on tools that reach this data beats a fine-tune on any data, and cannot be copied by anyone without the network. What follows is the tool surface, and the one rule that governs all of it.

Consent and scope live in the tool body. Always.

A prompt instruction is a suggestion to a language model. A check in the tool is a wall. Every tool takes org, actor and, for anything about a person, consent_ref — and validates them before it opens a connection. No tool in this catalogue writes. Riri advises; a human presses.

Three things to fix in the existing live paths before adding one more tool

  • Replace the substring entity check in AiIntelligenceController.RunGuardedQuery with a parsed predicate or, better, route it through the same catalogue-first path the console uses — a canonical question runs a pre-vetted metric and never reaches a model at all.
  • Give the Interchange node a login of its own. The suite connects today as service_suite_user / SystemUser1 — application logins that can write and can read every entity on a shared book. Create interchange_node with a view per entity, the EntityId baked inside the view, GRANT the schema and DENY everything else. Until that exists, every tool above is one query-generation bug away from crossing a tenant boundary.
  • Never hand her a bureau tool that is not pinned to salesmaster. A CRB call from an unregistered egress is dropped at Metropol's edge with no response and nothing in any log — it looks exactly like the bureau being down. The relay now enforces this by egress IP; the tool must not be able to choose a different relay.

Autopilot, across systems

Today Autopilot works and is well-judged: opt-in, navigation only, resolves a sentence to one of 65 screens, and stops at the door — it will take you to the disbursement screen and it will not press the button, because speech recognition mishears and the gap between “show me” and “send it” is one syllable. The extension is reach, not power.

The three rules that do not move

  • Navigation only. Autopilot moves the screen. It never submits a form, approves, disburses, sends money or sends a message. Crossing a system boundary does not change this — it makes it more important, because the user did not see the journey.
  • One destination or none. resolveDestination returns a single screen or refuses. An ambiguous cross-system match is offered as two buttons, never guessed — a wrong jump into another product is far more disorienting than a wrong jump within one.
  • A hand-off is a signed deep link with a reason. It carries the question that produced it and lands with a dismissible bar: “Riri brought you here from the console because you asked where Ratiba is set up.” Somebody who does not recognise where they are must always be able to read why they are there and get back.
07

The customer's Riri — first response

The whole point, stated plainly: there is no reason for a customer to wait for Geoffrey to be told they press *334#. Riri answers first, from the tenant corpus and the customer's own record, and only what she genuinely cannot resolve reaches the counter — arriving triaged, with her working shown.

The shape, borrowed from the Vercel agent

The reference screenshot gets one thing exactly right and it is not the styling: the agent opens by telling you what happens when it fails. “If we find something I can't solve, I'll help create a support case for you.” That sentence is what makes a customer willing to try the assistant at all, because the cost of it not working is stated up front and it is not “you wasted your afternoon”.

  1. Riri answers first, and says so

    The Messages composer opens with her line, her scope (“your loan, your repayments, your Ratiba, and how Micro Eazy works”) and the escape hatch. Nothing is created yet — a question that resolves never becomes a thread, and never becomes a number on Geoffrey's counter.

  2. She reads before she answers

    Tenant corpus for “what do you charge”. Her own record tools for “when is my next instalment” — customer.schedule, ratiba.status, customer.record, all self-scoped by the proven phone in borrowerFor(). The customer is never trusted for identity; that is already true of the portal and does not change.

  3. Three outcomes, and only one of them is a ticket

    Resolved — answered, thumbs-up/down, logged as a deflection. Resolved with an offer — answered, plus “want me to raise this with your officer anyway?”. Escalated — she opens the thread herself.

  4. An escalation arrives with the triage already done

    She writes the first message on the thread, in her own voice, addressed to the officer: what the customer asked, in their words; what she checked and what it said; which KB packs she matched and what she did not find; and the one thing she needs a human to decide. findOrOpenThread and postMessage already exist — the kind, the subject and the applicationId are hers to set, and the ownership check on applicationId stays exactly where it is.

  5. The counter learns from what it stops seeing

    Every deflection is a row: question, matched packs, confidence, outcome. A weekly view of “the ten questions Riri answered most” is the lender's own product backlog. A view of “the ten she escalated most” is the next ten KB entries somebody should write — and after they are written, the escalation rate for that cluster should fall. That is the only honest measure of whether any of this works.

What she must never do on this surface

  • State a figure she did not read from the record in this conversation. No estimates, ever.
  • Promise an outcome, a timeline or an approval. She may describe the process; she may not commit the lender to it.
  • Tell a customer their payment will go somewhere it will not. Micromart's RepaymentTrigger allocates to the loan first and the remainder to savings — a customer cannot choose, and an assistant that implies otherwise creates a dispute over real money.
  • Read back a full phone number or an ID number unless asked for it specifically.
  • Keep going when the customer asks for a person. “Let me get you to someone” is an instant, cheerful escalation — an assistant that argues about being replaced is the thing everyone hates about assistants.

Two standing rules this plan inherits

Never disrupt Micromart's shared server. Everything the AI layer adds is additive — a service on a port, a funnel mapping, a login with a view per entity. No iisreset, and check what shares an app pool before touching one. Never type a bare tailscale funnel off on these nodes: on salesmaster it silently kills the bureau link, on servicesuite it takes the live SQL path down and all six systems go amber at once. Always name the port.

One prerequisite worth stating on its own: the tailnet policy in relay-host/tailnet-policy.hujson is unapplied, and until it is, eight nodes hold a standing route to two lenders' live SQL Servers — which means Riri's careful tool boundary is bypassable by anyone who can open a TDS socket directly. Apply it in its own change window, after the cutover is green.

Each of these becomes a pinned corpus entry with authority: "platform" before she is allowed to answer in that area at all — because the failure mode is not silence, it is a confident, plausible, wrong number.

Decided, and what is left

Settled 11 September 2026

  • She is Riri, white-labelled per host. brand.ts now exports "Riri" plus an assistantName(host) seam; the ~40 call sites that read the constant flipped with it, and the ten hard-coded strings in the metric console now read it too. Internal identifiers did not move.
  • Her voice is ours, the facts are theirs, and the pack id is on every answer. sourceRef() in pack.ts is that decision in code.
  • A partner's Riri sees their own book and the Interchange under consent — not our system map, our metric definitions, or another member's packs. Enforced by the pack owner/audience fields and to be written into the agreement's schedule.
  • Escalations change shape, and that is a deliberate choice rather than something to discover in a month. Revisit at Sprint 2, when the deflection log has real rows.

The four below are what the decisions opened up rather than closed.

  1. Is production connecting as a NOBYPASSRLS role? answer first

    The §01 finding is measured in this environment only. If Vercel's DATABASE_URL also carries BYPASSRLS, tenant isolation on the live console is decoration. It is one query to settle and it outranks everything else on this page.

  2. When to run the re-seed

    The embedding path is pinned but the existing rows were written before it was. They are in the right space, so this is hygiene rather than a fix — worth doing in a quiet hour, with the seeder's new boot line captured, so there is a record of which model the corpus stands in.

  3. One name, or two done

    The console calls her ServiceSuite AI; the Hub and the C# module call her Riri. If she is to be sold into a competitor's console, the outward name should be brand-driven per host — one string, already isolated in brand.ts — while the persona stays constant. My recommendation: Riri is the product name, white-labelled per host, and the internal identifiers never change.

  4. Whether the customer's Riri is the lender's or ours

    A Micromart customer asking about a Micromart fee is answered from Micromart's pack. But whose voice apologises when she gets it wrong, and whose name is on the deflection report? My recommendation: her voice is ours, the facts are theirs, and the pack id is on every answer — which is also the only version that survives a dispute.

  5. How much of the estate a partner's Riri may see

    A lender hosting Riri gets their own book and the Interchange under consent. They do not get the system map of our console, our metric definitions or another member's packs. Worth writing into the agreement's schedule rather than leaving to the tool layer to imply.

  6. Whether escalations reach Geoffrey, or a queue

    Riri makes the counter smaller and sharper. It also changes what arrives: fewer, harder, pre-triaged. That is a different job from answering fifty “when is my due date” messages a day, and it is worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering it in a month.

What is built, and what was deliberately left

Sprint 0 is in the working tree of three repositories and is described in Table 10. Nothing was deployed, no relay was touched, and no query was run against any lender's live book. The two reads that did happen were against this console's own Postgres, were read-only, and were catalogue queries run to diagnose the RLS finding in §01.

Two Sprint 0 items are deliberately not done, because both are outward-facing and yours to trigger. The Supabase re-seed rewrites every row in a table the live Hub reads, so the code is pinned and the seeder now prints its model at boot, but it has not been run. And the six decisions the founder made on 11 Sep are implemented in code — Riri as the product name with a white-label seam, the pack id on every answer — while the Participation Agreement they imply (§08) is specified here and not yet drafted.
